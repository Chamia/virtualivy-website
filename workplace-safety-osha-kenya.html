Workplace Health & Safety in Kenya: The OSHA Rules SMEs Miss

June 2026 Virtual Ivy 6 min read

Say "OSHA" to most small-business owners in Kenya and they picture a factory floor, hard hats, heavy machinery, something that surely doesn't apply to a tidy office in Westlands or a shop in town. That assumption is the problem. The Occupational Safety and Health Act 2007 applies to every workplace, and the duties are more concrete than most SMEs realise.

What OSHA actually requires

The Act puts the responsibility on the "occupier", you, the employer, to provide a safe working environment: safe systems of work, training, first aid, fire safety, and protection from foreseeable risks. It doesn't matter whether your team is on a production line or behind laptops; the duty of care is the same.

Register your workplace with DOSHS

Under Section 44, anyone who occupies premises as a workplace must register it with the Directorate of Occupational Safety and Health Services (DOSHS). In practice that means filing the workplace registration form (DOSH 21A) and a self-assessment (DOSH 23). It's a step new businesses routinely skip, then scramble to fix during an inspection.

Safety committee: required at 20+ employees

If you regularly employ 20 or more people, you must set up a safety and health committee with fair representation from both staff and management, and it should meet quarterly. Smaller teams don't need a formal committee, but still carry the underlying safety duties.

Audits and accident reporting

Workplaces are expected to undergo periodic safety audits, and occupational accidents must be reported to DOSHS on the prescribed form within the statutory timeline. Keeping a simple incident record isn't just compliance, it's how you spot and fix hazards before they become claims.

Why a small business should care

Beyond the penalties and the liability that comes with an injured employee, safety is part of being a credible employer. Building it in early is far cheaper than retrofitting it after an incident. Frequently asked questions

Does OSHA apply to small offices in Kenya?
Yes. The Occupational Safety and Health Act 2007 applies to every workplace, including offices and shops, not just factories. The employer's duty to provide a safe working environment applies regardless of size or sector.
Do I need to register my workplace with DOSHS?
Yes. Section 44 requires anyone occupying premises as a workplace to register with the Directorate of Occupational Safety and Health Services, using the prescribed workplace registration and self-assessment forms.
When is a workplace safety committee required in Kenya?
When you regularly employ 20 or more people. The committee must have fair representation from workers and management and is expected to meet quarterly.
Do small businesses need a safety audit?
Workplaces are expected to undergo periodic safety audits and to report occupational accidents to DOSHS. Even very small teams carry the underlying duty to keep the workplace safe and to record incidents.
