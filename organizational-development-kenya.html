Build an organisation that performs

Organizational development is about making your organisation more effective: the right structure, a strong culture, capable leaders and smooth change. Virtual Ivy partners with Kenyan organisations to strengthen how they work, not just what they do.

What's included

  • Organisational structure design and review.
  • Culture and engagement initiatives.
  • Change management for restructures and growth.
  • Leadership and management development.
  • Capacity building and training.

Who it's for

Growing and established organisations, SMEs, NGOs and corporates, navigating change, scaling, or aiming to lift performance.

How our organizational development process works in Kenya

Good organizational development Kenya engagements are diagnostic before they are prescriptive. We start by listening, not reorganising. A typical engagement moves through a few clear stages so you always know what is happening and why.

  1. Discovery and diagnosis. We review your structure, roles, policies and people data, then talk to leaders and staff to understand where work actually flows and where it stalls.
  2. Findings and priorities. We share an honest picture of strengths, gaps and risks, and agree the two or three changes that will move the needle first.
  3. Design. Together we redesign reporting lines, role clarity, decision rights or the people practices that need attention, aligned to your HR strategy and business goals.
  4. Implementation support. We help you roll out the changes, brief managers, and adjust as reality bites.
  5. Embedding and review. We check what stuck, what slipped, and what to reinforce so the new way of working becomes the normal way of working.

The pace is set by your size and appetite for change. Some organisations want a focused structure review; others want a longer partnership covering workplace culture and capacity building over several months.

Change management that actually sticks

Most restructures fail not on the org chart but on people. Change management is the discipline of bringing your team with you, so new structures, systems or ways of working are adopted rather than resisted. We help you plan the human side deliberately.

  • A clear case for change that staff understand, in plain language, including what stays the same.
  • Honest, two-way communication through the transition, not a single announcement.
  • Manager enablement so the people leading teams can answer hard questions confidently.
  • Attention to fairness and process, consistent with the Employment Act 2007, where roles, terms or reporting lines change.

Handled well, change management protects morale, reduces avoidable turnover, and gets you to the benefits of the change sooner.

In-house or outsourced: choosing the right model

Organizational development can sit with an internal HR team, with an external partner, or somewhere in between. There is no single right answer, only the right fit for your stage.

  • Build in-house when you have the bandwidth and senior HR capability to lead change, and OD is a permanent priority. We can coach your team and transfer skills so the capacity stays with you.
  • Outsource when you need independent eyes, specialist experience, or simply do not have the internal time. An outside partner can also raise sensitive findings that are harder to surface from within.
  • Blend the two for most growing organisations: we lead the diagnosis and design, your managers own delivery, and capacity building leaves your people stronger than we found them.

If you are weighing this up, our broader HR consulting view can help you decide where external support adds the most value.

What organizational development costs

We do not publish fixed prices, because honest pricing depends on what you actually need. A short structure review for a 15-person SME is a very different piece of work from a multi-month change management and capacity building programme for a national NGO.

Cost is scoped to your size, stage and the depth of change involved. The main drivers are how many people and teams are affected, whether you need design only or full implementation support, and how long you want a partner alongside you. After a free consultation we give you a clear, written quote with defined deliverables, so there are no surprises and you can choose the scope that fits your budget.

Measuring whether it worked

Organizational development should produce results you can see, not just a tidy diagram. Before we begin, we agree what success looks like so progress is visible to your leadership and your team.

  • Clearer roles and decision-making, with fewer bottlenecks and less duplicated effort.
  • Stronger workplace culture and engagement, reflected in how people describe working with you.
  • Smoother change adoption, with managers confident and staff informed.
  • Real capacity building, measured by what your team can now do independently.

Because structure and people practices are connected, we often link OD outcomes to your performance management approach, so improved ways of working show up in how individuals and teams actually perform.

Organizational Development in Kenya: Frequently Asked Questions

What is organizational development?
Organizational development is the planned work of improving how an organisation performs: its structure, culture, leadership and ability to change. It goes beyond individual HR tasks to make the whole organisation more effective and adaptable.
When does a Kenyan business need OD support?
Typically during growth, restructuring, mergers, rapid hiring, or when culture and performance are slipping. If your structure or ways of working no longer fit your size, OD helps you redesign them deliberately rather than by accident.
What does an OD engagement actually deliver?
Depending on scope: a redesigned organisational structure, clearer roles and reporting lines, a performance or competency framework, change-management support, and a plan to build leadership and team capability.
How do you measure the success of OD work?
Against outcomes agreed up front, clearer accountability, reduced turnover, faster decisions, improved engagement, and progress on the business goals the work was tied to. We define the measures with you before we start.
