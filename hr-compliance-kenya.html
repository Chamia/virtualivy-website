Stay compliant, avoid costly mistakes

Non-compliance is one of the biggest hidden risks for Kenyan employers, from poorly drafted contracts to missed statutory deductions and unlawful dismissals. Virtual Ivy makes sure your people practices are compliant, documented and defensible.

The main areas of compliance in Kenya

Employment compliance in Kenya is governed primarily by the Employment Act 2007, alongside the Labour Relations Act, the Occupational Safety and Health Act, and the statutory schemes for tax and social security. In practice, it comes down to getting a handful of areas right and keeping them current as the law changes.

Contracts and documentation. Every employee is entitled to a written contract that sets out their terms, pay, hours, leave and notice. Missing or outdated contracts are the most common, and most avoidable, source of disputes.

Statutory deductions. Employers must register for and correctly remit PAYE to KRA, NSSF contributions, SHIF (which replaced NHIF), and the Affordable Housing Levy. Rates and schemes change, so payroll has to be reviewed regularly, not set once and forgotten.

Working time, leave and pay. Annual, sick, maternity and paternity leave are statutory entitlements with specific rules. Getting accrual, pay and record-keeping right protects you if a claim is ever raised.

Discipline and termination. There is no "at-will" employment in Kenya. Both a valid reason and a fair, documented procedure are required, and redundancy has its own distinct process. This is where employers most often lose unfair-dismissal claims.

Records and data. The law expects employers to keep proper personnel records. Good documentation is both a compliance requirement and your best protection in a dispute.

What's included

  • HR compliance audit, where you stand against the law.
  • Employment contracts and offer letters per the Employment Act 2007.
  • Statutory compliance, PAYE, NSSF, SHIF and the Affordable Housing Levy.
  • HR policies and handbook, disciplinary, grievance, leave, health and safety.
  • Disciplinary and termination support, fair and lawful processes.

Common compliance gaps we find

When we audit a business, the same issues come up again and again across Kenyan employers:

  • Employees working without signed, up-to-date contracts.
  • Policies and handbooks that pre-date SHIF and the Affordable Housing Levy.
  • Statutory deductions calculated or filed incorrectly, or filed late.
  • Disciplinary action and dismissals taken without a fair, documented procedure.
  • Incomplete employee records, contracts, leave, and disciplinary history.

Each of these is inexpensive to fix early and expensive to fix once it becomes a dispute or a penalty. An audit turns "we think we're fine" into a clear, prioritised plan.

Who needs a compliance review

If you are hiring your first employees, scaling past ten staff, preparing for investor or donor due diligence, or simply have not reviewed your contracts and policies in over a year, a focused review is worth it. It is also the right first step before restructuring, redundancies, or any significant change to your workforce.

Why it matters

Getting compliance wrong can mean disputes, penalties and reputational damage. Getting it right protects your business and gives your team confidence. We translate complex labour law into clear, practical steps.

FAQ

HR Compliance in Kenya: Frequently Asked Questions

What does HR compliance in Kenya involve?
It covers written contracts that meet the Employment Act 2007, lawful policies and procedures, accurate statutory deductions (PAYE, NSSF, SHIF, Housing Levy), proper record-keeping, and fair disciplinary and termination practice.
How do I know if my business is compliant?
The fastest way is an HR audit, a focused review of your contracts, policies, payroll and records against the law. It produces a prioritised, risk-rated list of exactly what to fix.
What are the risks of non-compliance?
Common consequences include back-pay claims, unfair-dismissal awards, penalties for incorrect statutory remittances, and reputational damage. Most are avoidable with the right setup.
Can you fix gaps as well as identify them?
Yes. We do not just hand you a report, we implement the fixes: drafting compliant contracts and policies, correcting payroll, and putting proper records in place.
